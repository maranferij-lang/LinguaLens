Коротко

LinguaLens не збирає ім’я, пошту чи контакти, не показує рекламу і не відстежує тебе між застосунками. Без входу слова, прогрес і фото наліпок зберігаються лише на твоєму телефоні. Вхід через Apple необов’язковий: він потрібен лише для того, щоб словник мав резервну копію й був однаковий на всіх твоїх iPhone. Фото, яке ти скануєш, іде сторонньому AI-сервісу (Google Gemini або Anthropic) лише для розпізнавання, і ми його не зберігаємо. Анонімну статистику використання можна вимкнути в Параметрах. Свій запис на нашому сервері ти стираєш однією кнопкою в застосунку.

Контролер даних (той, хто вирішує, навіщо й як їх обробляти): розробник LinguaLens, названий продавцем на сторінці застосунку в App Store.

Навіщо й на якій підставі

Якщо до тебе застосовується європейський закон про захист даних (GDPR), ми обробляємо дані на таких підставах:

  • Договір (ст. 6(1)(b)). Щоб застосунок робив те, про що ти просиш: анонімний запис, розпізнавання скану, слово дня, синхронізація словника після входу через Apple, статус підписки.

What we process

  • An anonymous device identifier. On first launch our server creates a random id. It is used to count your free scan (one in total), pick your word of the day and link a subscription to the device. Unless you sign in with Apple, it isn’t tied to your name or Apple ID.
  • Sign in with Apple (optional). If you sign in, Apple gives us a stable identifier for your Apple ID in our app. We store only an anonymised hash of it, to recognise your account on your other iPhones. We never store your name or email (we don’t ask for them). We also keep a token from Apple so we can revoke the sign-in when you erase your account.
  • Your synced word list (only when signed in). Your words, transcriptions, translations, examples, languages and review schedule, plus daily activity counts, achievement counters and which achievements you’ve already seen, are stored on our server (Google Cloud) so you can use them on all your iPhones. Sticker photos and the name and avatar in your profile never leave the phone. When you delete a word, the server keeps only a marker with its internal id and time for up to 60 days, so the deletion reaches your other iPhones.
  • Scan counts and Pro subscription status. How many scans and how many whole-room scans you have made in total (for the free scan and the free try), and the expiry date of your Pro subscription.
  • Your learning profile from your answers in the app: goals, field, level and where you heard about us (no personal data). It is used to pick your words of the day and to count where people come from; “Erase all my data” deletes it. The list of words you marked “I know it” is sent with each word-of-the-day request so they can be skipped; the server doesn’t store it. An AI service translates the word of the day, but we ask it to translate a word from our own list: there is no data about you in that request.
  • The camera frame when you scan. A downscaled photo is sent to our server and on to a third-party AI provider (Google Gemini API or Anthropic API) only to recognise the object. We don’t store these photos. The AI provider may keep the request for a limited time to detect abuse and doesn’t use it to train its models. It receives the photo without our identifier. The cut-out sticker stays on your device only.
  • Purchases. Payments are processed by Apple. We receive the subscription status via RevenueCat, a subscription management service. We never see your card details. If you installed the app from an Apple Ads ad, RevenueCat receives the campaign from Apple (Apple Ads attribution), without any advertising identifier and without tracking you across apps.
  • Anonymous usage statistics. To understand what helps and what gets in the way, the app sends short events to PostHog (EU servers, Frankfurt): which onboarding steps were completed and which options were picked from fixed lists (goals, field, level, what gets in the way of learning, where you heard about us), whether the subscription screen was shown and a purchase started, whether a scan worked, how many words were saved, what was shared, and when the app was opened or sent to the background. Events include the app language, the language you’re learning, your level, whether you have Pro, the phone’s language and time zone, screen size, app version and a random install id created by PostHog itself. That id isn’t linked to our device id, your Apple ID or your purchases. We never send your name, email, photos or the words in your dictionary to analytics. PostHog doesn’t store your IP address or derive your country or city from it, and there is no screen recording. Events are kept for up to 1 year. To turn it off: Settings → Anonymous statistics.
  • IP address and server logs. Our server sees the IP address of every request. We use it only to limit abuse (rate limits) and keep it in the server’s memory, not in the database. Google Cloud, where the server runs, also writes it to its standard request logs. Our own log lines contain no photo and no identifier of yours: only technical details such as the time, the languages, the recognised word and how long the request took.

Why we use your data, and on what basis

If European data protection law (GDPR) applies to you, for example if you are in the EU, we use these legal bases:

  • Contract (Art. 6(1)(b)). To give you the service you ask for: the anonymous record, recognising your scan, the word of the day, syncing your word list after Sign in with Apple, and your subscription status.
  • Legitimate interests (Art. 6(1)(f)). Anonymous usage statistics, to improve the app. You can turn them off in Settings, and then the app sends nothing. Also protecting the service from abuse: request limits per IP address and a free scan count that erasing your data does not reset.
  • Consent (Art. 6(1)(a)). Where the app asks for it, for example the iOS permissions for the camera, notifications or photos. You can withdraw it in iOS Settings. This doesn’t affect what was done before.

What we don’t collect

  • Name, email, phone number, contacts, location. The name you enter during onboarding (“What should we call you?”) stays on your phone.
  • Advertising identifiers (IDFA) or tracking data. There are no ads in the app, and we don’t share data with ad networks or data brokers.
  • Screen recordings, or your IP address in analytics.
  • Without Sign in with Apple, your word list and progress (streaks, achievements) never leave your phone. Sticker photos and the name and avatar in your profile always stay on the device.
  • The photo you pick as a Stories background and the scan frame used as one never leave your phone: only Instagram receives them, and only when you tap the button yourself.

Third parties and where your data goes

  • Google Cloud: our server (Cloud Run) and database (Firestore). They run in Warsaw (Poland, EU).
  • Google Gemini API or Anthropic API (one of the two): image recognition. It receives the downscaled photo and the languages, without our identifier. It may process data in the US and other countries.
  • RevenueCat (US): subscription management. It receives a random device id, purchase data from Apple and, if you came from an Apple Ads ad, the campaign attribution.
  • Apple: payments, Sign in with Apple, Apple Ads. Apple is responsible for this data under its own privacy policy.
  • PostHog: anonymous usage statistics, EU servers (Frankfurt).

Some of them (Google Gemini, Anthropic, RevenueCat, Apple) may process data outside the EU and the EEA, including in the US. They then rely on safeguards the EU recognises: Standard Contractual Clauses or the EU-US Data Privacy Framework. We don’t sell data.

How long we keep data

  • Scan photos: not stored. They exist only in the server’s memory while recognition runs. The AI service may keep the request for a limited time to detect abuse.
  • Your anonymous device record (id, a random number for the word of the day, scan counts, subscription status, learning profile): until you erase it in the app. We don’t delete inactive records automatically.
  • Your Apple account, synced word list and Apple token: until you erase your account. Markers of deleted words: up to 60 days.
  • Google Cloud request logs (with the IP address): up to 30 days, which is the Google Cloud default.
  • Anonymous statistics in PostHog: up to 1 year.
  • Purchase records at Apple and RevenueCat: under their own rules.

We keep no name or email, so we can’t look up your record by name. You can see and edit your words and progress in the app, and you can erase everything yourself. For any other request, write to us. If we can’t tell which record is yours, we will say so and explain what else we need. We answer within one month.

Deleting your data

Settings → Data → “Erase all my data”. This deletes the record on our server (scan counts, subscription status and learning profile) and everything on the phone. Only the number of scans already made carries over to this phone’s new anonymous record (with no link to the erased one), so that erasing doesn’t give a new free scan. If you signed in with Apple, it also deletes your account and the synced word list from our server, and we revoke the Sign in with Apple authorization. Your other iPhones keep what’s already on them but stop syncing. Subscriptions are cancelled in your Apple ID settings: erasing doesn’t cancel one.

This button doesn’t delete purchase records at Apple and RevenueCat. If you want those deleted too, write to us.

“Sign out” in Settings → Account clears only this phone: your word list stays in your account and comes back when you sign in again.

The “Anonymous statistics” switch in Settings turns analytics off: after that the app sends nothing to PostHog. Events already sent aren’t linked to you, so we can’t pick out yours among them.

Children

LinguaLens is not meant for children under 13, and we don’t knowingly collect their personal data. If the law in your country sets a higher age, that age applies. If you think a child has given us data, write to us and we will delete it.
